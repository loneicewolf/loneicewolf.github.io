# On AI Hate, Harassment, and Where the Line Is

Disliking AI tools-or feeling skeptical about AI-generated art is a normal opinion to have. People debate creativity, authorship, training data, and quality all the time, and disagreement itself isn't the problem.

The problem starts when disagreement turns into **targeted hostility, pile-ons, and harassment**.

---

## Why "Slop" and Name-Calling Miss the Mark

Words like *"slop"* or *"slopper"* get used a lot online as a shortcut to dismiss anything AI-related. Usually, the people using them feel they are defending "real" art or calling out low effort.

In practice, insulting the person rarely changes anyone's mind:
* **It skips the actual debate.** Calling something "slop" is a label, not an argument about what makes good art or why a given piece falls short.
* **It targets a real person.** Behind a screen, there is still a human being reading that message-often someone using a tool out of curiosity, convenience, or accessibility, not to attack anyone else.
* **It lowers the level of the conversation.** Mocking someone for how they create doesn't protect human creativity; it just makes online spaces more hostile for everyone in them.

---

## The "AI Isn't Art" Argument

A common claim is that AI output simply isn't art. The trouble is that **art itself doesn't have a single, universally agreed-upon definition**-it depends heavily on intent, context, taste, and personal meaning.

If someone defines art strictly by traditional human brush-to-canvas effort, that's one lens. Still, treating all AI use as worthless or bad faith ignores how widely AI is used-from creative experimentation to tools that help people work, learn, or pass time when they're stuck in tough situations like long medical treatments or recovery. You don't have to call every AI image fine art to admit that attacking people over it is unnecessary.

---

## Disagreement vs. Harassment

There is a big difference between:
* Critiquing a tool, a trend, or an image, versus
* Hunting down individuals, dogpiling them in comments or DMs, or sending threats.

Targeted harassment and cyber-stalking cross a clear ethical-and often legal-line. Even when someone feels strongly about a topic, taking pleasure in making someone else miserable isn't "justice" or standing up for what's right.

Nobody gets everything right online, and we all say things we regret sometimes. Real accountability doesn't require perfection; it just means being willing to step back, recognize when a line was crossed, and choose not to pile on.

---

## Choosing Not to Escalate

When people get hit with hostility online, it's tempting to answer hate with hate just to fight back or fit into the cycle. Stepping away from that cycle-refusing to become cruel yourself and focusing on what you actually enjoy making-takes more patience, and it keeps your own values intact.

You don't have to love AI to treat other people like human beings. Criticize the work if you want, log off if you don't like it, and leave the personal attacks out of it.
